How eBay's fees work in 2026

eBay does not charge one fee. On a normal US sale it charges a final value fee set by category, a per-order fee, and — if you advertise or sell abroad — a promoted listings fee and an international fee. This calculator uses the rates published on eBay's own selling-fees page on 20 November 2026; the full table, with sources, is in the paid workbook's Fee Watch sheet.

Two things most calculators get wrong. Sales tax is inside the fee base — eBay charges its percentage on the tax it collected. And a category is either marginal or whole-amount: jewelry is 15% of the whole sale at $5,000 or less and 9% of the whole sale above it, so $5,000.01 pays $300 less than $5,000.00.

Questions

Why is the fee higher than 13.6% + $0.40?

Because the final value fee is charged on the shipping the buyer paid as well as the item, and the ad rate is charged on that same total. On a $59.99 item with $9.30 shipping and a 5% ad rate, the final value fee is $9.42, the per-order fee $0.40 and the ad fee $3.46 — $13.28, or 19.2% of what the buyer paid.

My category is not in the list.

The thirteen here cover what resellers actually sell. eBay publishes a rate for every leaf category on its selling-fees page; pick the closest one above and treat the number as an estimate, or change the rate in the paid workbook's Fee Watch sheet, where the whole file follows.

Is jewelry really cheaper above $5,000?

Yes. Jewelry (watches excluded) is whole-amount: 15% of the entire sale at $5,000 or less, and 9% of the entire sale above it. Crossing the line by one cent drops the fee by about $300. It is the only US category that pays you to raise the price.

What about store subscription fees?

This tool shows the rate card a Store unlocks and what it saves on this one order — it does not charge the subscription to the order. In the paid workbook the subscription is counted against the fee savings across all your orders, which is the only way to know whether the Store pays for itself.

Does the shipping I charge count as revenue?

Yes, and it is feeable. eBay charges its final value fee on shipping, so "free shipping" you absorb is a real cost on top of the label.

How current are the rates?
